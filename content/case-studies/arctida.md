## Giving research a durable publishing home

Arctida is a nonprofit organisation working on analysis and investigations concerning the Russian Arctic. The website brings together investigations, reports, analytical materials and special projects for audiences with different levels of familiarity with the region.

The development challenge is to make a growing publication library usable. A reader needs to recognise the type of material, understand its subject and move from a featured story into related work.

## Multiple routes into the same library

The navigation separates publication formats, while thematic links offer another way to explore the collection. The homepage highlights individual stories and provides routes into investigations and analytical materials.

Article pages carry the longer reading experience. Consistent presentation gives the material a shared structure while preserving the images, subject matter and publication context of each piece.

![Arctida publication library with research formats and article cards](/case-studies/arctida-materials.webp)

## Editorial content and specialised tools

The implementation combines a Next.js website with Sanity content management. Publication content and its relationships live in the CMS; reusable page components present that content across the site.

The site also introduces specialised research tools, including Arctida Stakeholders. Giving those tools visible entry points allows the publishing website to connect an explanatory article with a more exploratory experience.

Russian and English versions, subscription forms and contact routes support both reading and continued engagement with the organisation.

## The result

Arctida has a public publishing site that accommodates both long-form editorial work and links to interactive research. The case demonstrates a content architecture built around publication formats, themes and related projects.
